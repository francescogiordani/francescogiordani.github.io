Every cell has its own source. The small ↗ after a value opens the exact page that states it, so nothing here has to be taken on trust. A value with a dotted underline came from my own Fall 2026 spreadsheet and has not been re-read on the page today. A cell reading "not checked" means I had no source I was willing to link, which is deliberate: an empty cell is better than an unverifiable one. Acceptance rate is mostly empty on purpose. Very few departments publish one. A value with a dashed underline comes from an unofficial source, linked like everything else, and should be read as an estimate. I deliberately did not copy the percentages that circulate on ranking and admissions-consultancy sites: those are institution-wide or undergraduate figures, and where I could check one against a department's own numbers it was nearly twice as high.

The chip refers to the application call only. Slower-moving columns (housing, insurance, stipend) are governed by the "checked" date on the row.

What the columns mean
University
Which institution the row is about. The chip underneath says whether the row reflects the published Fall 2027 call or the previous cycle.
Program
The exact degree you would apply to. One university often runs several relevant programmes with different deadlines, which is why Carnegie Mellon appears twice.
Deadline
The date the application closes, with the local cut-off time where the source states one. The "checked" line underneath is when I last read that page.
Acceptance rate
The share of applicants admitted. Mostly empty on purpose: very few departments publish one, and the percentages on ranking sites are undergraduate figures.
English
Which English test the programme accepts and the score it asks for, or the scores its admitted students reported.
Housing
Whether the university offers graduate housing, and whether you apply for it separately from admission.
Health
What medical cover a funded student gets, who pays for it, and whether it runs for the whole year.
Links
The pages this row was read from, so any value can be checked at its source rather than taken on trust.
GREUSA only
Whether the general GRE is required, optional, ignored, or not accepted at all for this cycle.
FundingUSA only
Whether admitted PhD students are funded by the department as standard, rather than left to find their own support.
Dental & visionUSA only
Whether dental and vision cover is included in the health plan, offered as a paid add-on, or absent.
Funding modelEurope only
How the position is paid for: an employment contract with a salary, a scholarship or stipend, or fees you pay yourself.
Pay
What you are actually paid to live on, over the period the source quotes, which is often 9 or 10 months rather than 12. Whether that arrives as a salary or as a stipend is what the Funding model column says.
Application fee
What it costs to submit the application, quoted for an international applicant where the university charges a different amount. Across most of continental Europe it is nothing.

The two tables share most columns. Anything tagged USA only or Europe only appears in just one of them.

Europe

9 programs

Europe does not work like the USA, so the columns are different. There is usually no application fee and no GRE. In Switzerland and Germany a doctorate is a job: you sign an employment contract and draw a salary rather than a stipend. Deadlines are often rolling, and admission frequently comes from a single professor with an opening rather than from a departmental committee. The UK sits in between, with fees, gathered fields instead of one deadline, and scholarships that must be won separately. ELLIS is not a university: it is a Europe-wide network with one central application that matches you to advisors in different countries, and its deadline is the earliest of anything on this page.

Timeline

autumn 2026 to spring 2027
  • September 2026. Shortlist programs. Ask for letters now, not in November. Give each referee a CV, a draft statement, a bullet list of what you did together, and the deadline table.
  • October 2026. Sit IELTS or TOEFL if the score is not already in hand. Most applications open this month. 31 October is the ELLIS deadline, a full month before anything else, and it needs a two-page motivation letter plus two referees already lined up. Plan backwards from this date, not from December.
  • November 2026. Statement of purpose drafts, one fit paragraph per school. Order transcripts.
  • 1 to 16 December 2026. Nearly everything is due. In this table the cluster is 1 December (MIT, Stanford, Berkeley, UIUC, EPFL), then 9 December (CMU CSD and TTIC), then 15 to 16 December (Princeton, Cornell, UW, Georgia Tech, UT Austin, UCLA, UCSD), then 31 December (Max Planck).
  • Early January 2027. 8 January is ISTA, with referee letters due 12 January. UCL runs on its own rolling two-cycle calendar and wants a research proposal and a named supervisor, so it cannot be left to the last week.
  • January to March 2027. Interviews and open houses. EPFL flies December-round fellowship candidates in.
  • March to April 2027. Decisions. 15 April is the shared reply deadline at US schools, so you are not obliged to answer the first offer immediately.
  • Any month. ETH and KTH sit outside this calendar entirely. Neither runs a central call: you apply to a named professor or to a specific advertised position. KTH posts positions nine times a year, so it is worth checking through the autumn rather than once.
  • Spring and summer 2027. I-20, SEVIS fee, visa interview, then travel.

English tests

logistics
  • Validity is two years. A test sat in early 2025 can expire before a December 2026 submission. UCLA states this explicitly.
  • IELTS vs TOEFL. Both are accepted everywhere in this table. MIT states a preference for IELTS. Duolingo is accepted by some (Princeton, UCSD) and not by others, and the number accepting it has fallen since 2023.
  • A waiver is unlikely. An Italian MSc, even one taught in English, usually does not earn an exemption at US schools. MIT's waiver has specific conditions worth reading rather than assuming.
  • Score reports take one to two weeks to reach institutions and cost extra per report. Book the sitting so results land well before 1 December.

Applying from an Italian degree

paperwork
  • Transcripts plus the Diploma Supplement, in English. Order early, Italian administrative offices are slow in the autumn.
  • Do not try to convert 110/110 into a US GPA yourself unless a school asks. Report the Italian scale and let them read it. Most systems have a field for this.
  • Letters from Italian professors are often shorter and more formal than the US norm. It is worth telling your referees that US committees expect specifics and comparisons, not a character reference.

Visa and permits

the most volatile section here
  • USA. F-1 after the I-20 arrives, then the SEVIS I-901 fee and the DS-160, then an appointment in Milan, Rome, Florence or Naples. Wait times move constantly, so check them rather than trusting any page, including this one. Administrative processing under 221(g) is a genuine possibility in CS and AI fields, so leave slack.
  • Switzerland. A doctorate is employment, so this runs as a B permit through the employer.
  • UK. Several CS research areas require an ATAS certificate before the visa, which adds weeks and is easy to miss. Check it early for Oxford, Cambridge and UCL.
